I had ran the previous design of my blog for three years, which I *think* is the longest I have ever held onto a design. But I felt that a redesign was needed to refocus the blog, and to keep things fresh. So, here we are; version 6.

## Updated packages

Plenty of packages including NextJS have been updated. I had been falling behind on keeping these up to date so I figured it was a good opportunity to update them along with a redesign.

## Improved homepage

The homepage has had a makeover. The introductory paragraph is now much shorter, more concise, and overall more to the point. The links remains as it’s a good way for visitors to learn more about me. There are also two new buttons, that lead a visitor to the blog and contact pages.

The five most recent blog posts are now highlighted on the front page. Now visitors can get a feel for what I write about without having to navigate to the blog page. Each post is presented in a neat card with a hover over effect if you’re on a desktop.

The polaroid image has also been improved. The polaroid is no longer an image, it’s CSS. This cuts down the size of the image as a whole. Plus, the polaroid frame now changes depending if light or dark mode is used.

## Changes to the blog page

The pinned post feature now has it’s own dedicated card, with a hover over effect if you’re on a desktop. I think the card looks neater and grabs attention more than the separate heading I had previously. 

The date when the post was published and the reading time have been moved to the same row as the title. Now each post doesn’t take up as much room. Also, there is some new fonts and colours used to focus the readers attention on the title, then subtitle and then the published date and reading time. This all helps make the experience for the reader better.

The pagination has also been improved; it now no longer shows all the pages on the blog via a scrollbar! There’s now so much less being shown which is great and users can navigate to the first page or last page.

## Blog post improvements

For desktop users, there is now a new section for blog posts, the ‘on this page’ section. Visitors can now easily jump to different sections of the page where I have used a heading in the markdown file. Just click on the heading you want to skip to and the browser will scroll down to that heading. This feature is not available on the mobile view.

The tags for a post have also been moved from the bottom of a post, to the top. It makes sense to me to keep the metadata of a post in one section at the top, rather than the bottom. Speaking of the tags, these have been redesigned to a pill design.

## Resumé

The biggest, ground breaking change, is that ‘Resume’ has been changed to ‘Resumé’. I decided I didn’t want people get confused with the verb, ‘resume’.

The overall layout hasn’t changed, but several things have been cleaned up. The pills that are used to describe a skill have been changed to a more modern look.

The polaroid images use the same changes that I mentioned on the homepage.

## Contact changes

The SVG icons have been dropped, and the new card design has been used for each link. Very simple, but for a contact page, it’s what’s needed.

## Global changes

There’s a few new colours being used, primarily a rust orange accent. You’ll find this as a hover over effect, links and in a few other spots. I’m a fan of orange, so I thought giving it a spin might be fun. The colour is slightly different depending if you are viewing the website on light or dark mode.

There are also new fonts! The main font is [instrument sans](https://github.com/Instrument/instrument-sans) and the other font used for metadata etc is [spline sans mono](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Spline+Sans+Mono?preview.script=Latn). Both look really great and it makes the overall site look more professional.

The header and footer have also been reworked. The font is smaller, and the SVGs have been dropped. It looks better, cleaner and doesn’t get in the way of the content itself.

The pill design is now used more consistently across the whole site where it is appropriate. Such as tags, the dark/light mode button, and skills.

## A long journey in design and writing

This blog has come a *long* way since I originally had it up and running on Jekyll, then moving over to Gatsby (and having a *horrific* design along with an incredibly long config file that was impossible to maintain) to having something more modern and easier to maintain.

I was tempted to move over to a service like [Ghost](https://ghost.org/), but I still enjoy having my own space where I can do whatever I want. I’ve touched on this subject [before](https://www.joshblewitt.dev/posts/2024-06-04-why-website) and I view it like a home where I can knock down walls and get an extension.

When I started this blog in 2019, I wasn’t sure how long I was going to keep it around for. Maybe I’d post on it once or twice and then forget about it. But I didn’t, I kept writing, posting, and maintaining this blog. I’d like to think that overtime, my writing skills have improved somewhat. My posts are now more structured, detailed and researched than they were back in 2019. Writing has now become one of my favourite hobbies, and I’ve now come to enjoy writing my end of year posts as it gives me time to stop and think on what’s happened over the past year.

Another benefit of this website is that it has helped me secure job interviews and job offers. Recruiters like seeing these websites, it gives them an excuse to pick up the phone and call you. So if you’re on the fence about starting a website of your own, my advice is to do it and give it a shot. Because it might be the start of your favourite hobby.
